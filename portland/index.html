Keeping Your Portland Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Long before a tank floods a Portland garage, it usually shows warning signs. Look out for rumbling and popping while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, moisture around the base, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
