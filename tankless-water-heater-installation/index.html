Why do so many people switch to tankless? Mostly three things: hot water that keeps coming, lower standby energy use, and space. A tankless heater stays idle until you open a tap, then fires a high-output burner or electric element to heat water as it flows. With no stored water to keep warm, it wastes less energy between uses, and with regular descaling it can last twenty years or more. It is not magic, though. Performance depends entirely on sizing and installation, which is where most of our work in Beaverton, OR goes.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We work from two numbers, flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver far less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw several times the gas of a standard tank, which often means a larger gas line or a meter check. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

When we install a tankless heater, we build it to be serviced. That means isolation valves for descaling, a gas line with a sediment trap sized for the burner's full demand, venting that meets the manufacturer's rules for length and termination, and a condensate line with a neutralizer on condensing models. Where water hardness is high, we explain scale protection options before the unit goes on the wall. Optional recirculation can shorten the wait at distant fixtures. We handle permits where required, test the unit at full flow, and show you how it works. Homeowners in Beaverton can call (503) 210-1616 for a demand assessment and a clear quote.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Could a handy homeowner install tankless? Because the risks and the details stack up quickly. Gas piping must be sized for a burner that may draw nearly 199,000 BTU, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. Electric models may need a panel upgrade, and warranty terms often depend on a qualified installation. Permits and inspections are common requirements as well.

Done professionally, a tankless system performs well and stays safe. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, then a test with several fixtures running. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The right fit matters more to us than the type of unit.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • A setting near 120F limits scalding and reduces scale formation
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • In freezing weather, keep the unit powered and insulate exposed piping
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
