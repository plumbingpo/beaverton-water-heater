Many of the installation calls we get in Beaverton, OR are not replacements at all. A household outgrows its tank and needs more capacity. A homeowner wants the heater out of a hall closet and into the garage or basement. A remodel calls for a second unit at the far end of the house, or an all-electric conversion means trading gas for a heat pump model. Every one of them involves new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Beaverton Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Good installations start on paper. Tanks are sized by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at your water's temperature rise, so the heater keeps pace with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. A gas unit needs a vent path to the outside and enough combustion air. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Electric tankless units can need a heavy dedicated circuit and the panel capacity behind it. A heater in an attic or above finished space needs a drain pan piped to a safe spot. We talk it all through in plain language and then give you a firm price.

On installation day, every step gets verified. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. For electric and heat pump units, the circuit is matched to the load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. When to Call for Water Heater Installation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Remodel plans that add fixtures or a second bathroom
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines must be sized for the total load on the system and tested for leaks. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits that match their load, sometimes more than an older panel can supply. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. We size by first-hour rating or flow rate, run and test the gas piping, route the vent to spec, match the circuit to the load, and add expansion tanks, drain pans, or straps where required. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
