Two quiet processes decide how long a water heater lasts: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they steal efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Neither one is visible from the outside, and by the time a homeowner notices, the tank is often leaking. That is the reason maintenance matters for Beaverton, OR homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

A Beaverton Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we test the elements and thermostats with a meter and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually setting off error codes. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Beaverton Water Heater at (503) 210-1616 for a prompt inspection.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
