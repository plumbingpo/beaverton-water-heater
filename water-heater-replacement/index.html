When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. A correctly rated relief valve has to be piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, you will hear it from us directly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
